The campaign

A tournament of absolute mastery

Opening statement
122* vs South Africa — Southampton

Rohit began the World Cup with an unbeaten 122 against South Africa at the Rose Bowl. Leading a tricky chase, he reached his 23rd ODI century in 128 balls, hitting 10 fours and two sixes. India won by six wickets with 15 balls to spare. That day he also crossed 12,000 international runs and leapfrogged Sourav Ganguly on the all-time ODI centuries list[reference:0].

The Pakistan masterclass
140 vs Pakistan — Old Trafford

In front of a global audience of nearly a billion viewers, Rohit produced what many consider his finest ODI innings. Asked to bat first, he took the attack apart with 14 fours and three sixes, scoring 140 off just 113 balls. He forged a 136-run stand with KL Rahul and a 98-run partnership with Virat Kohli. He brought up his 24th ODI ton off just 85 deliveries. One shot — a six over backward point — drew immediate comparisons to Sachin Tendulkar's famous six against Pakistan in the 2003 World Cup[reference:1][reference:2].

"Rohit was at his elegant best at Old Trafford as he brought up his 24th ODI ton off just 85 deliveries before shifting up a gear."

The consecutive hundreds
102 vs England · 104 vs Bangladesh · 103 vs Sri Lanka

Rohit became only the second player in World Cup history — after Kumar Sangakkara in 2015 — to score three consecutive centuries in the tournament. He scored 102 against England at Edgbaston, 104 against Bangladesh, and 103 against Sri Lanka. Along with KL Rahul, he also registered the highest-ever opening partnership for India in a World Cup: 189 against Sri Lanka[reference:3].

The records
A campaign that rewrote the record book

Rohit finished as the tournament's leading run-scorer with 648 runs in nine matches at an average of 81. His five centuries were the most by any player in a single edition of the World Cup, surpassing Sangakkara's four in 2015. His total World Cup centuries tally reached six, a record he shares with Sachin Tendulkar — though Rohit reached it in just 14 innings compared to Tendulkar's 44. He also earned four Man of the Match awards, equalling the record held by Aravinda de Silva, Lance Klusener and Yuvraj Singh [reference:4].

The heartbreak
Semi-final exit against New Zealand

Despite topping the group stage, India's campaign ended in the semi-final against New Zealand. Rohit later reflected on the campaign with mixed emotions: "2019 World Cup was the best cricket I have played, got some runs but again I have mentioned many times if you don't win the trophy, these runs don't really matter. I was disappointed as we have given everything as a team but it wasn't just for us."[reference:5]

The campaign

A captain's redemption

The batting masterclass
92 vs Australia — Super 8

Rohit later called this his best-ever T20I innings. Against a formidable Australian attack, he produced a blistering knock that set the tone for India's campaign. It was the kind of fearless, selfless batting that defined his captaincy throughout the tournament[reference:6].

The final — Barbados
29 June 2024 · India vs South Africa

India posted 176/7, built around Virat Kohli's 76 — the highest score by an Indian in a T20 World Cup final — and a crucial partnership with Axar Patel, who scored 47 off 31 balls. Kohli and Axar's rescue act steadied the innings after early wickets[reference:7].

Defending 177, India seemed in control until Heinrich Klaasen launched a counterattack, taking 24 runs off one Axar over. At that moment, South Africa needed just 30 runs off 30 balls. Rohit later admitted: "Yes, I was completely blank."[reference:8]

The death overs
Five overs that changed everything

What followed was a masterclass in pressure bowling. Jasprit Bumrah conceded just four runs in the 16th over. Hardik Pandya dismissed Klaasen in the 17th. Arshdeep Singh gave away only four in the 19th, leaving South Africa needing 16 off the final over. Hardik Pandya then defended it, including the wicket of David Miller, sealing a seven-run victory[reference:9].

The moment
The collapse, the tears, the trophy

When the final ball was bowled, Rohit collapsed to the ground in relief and jubilation — a moment now immortalised in images and videos. He later shared a sentimental video montage, including him lifting the trophy with his family [reference:10]. Reflecting on the victory, he said: "It was by far the best moment of my life, of my cricketing career — to lift that trophy." [reference:11]

Retirement
The end of a T20I era

Shortly after the final, Rohit announced his retirement from T20 internationals, walking away as the format's leading run-scorer and six-hitter, with a World Cup trophy in his hands and a legacy secured[reference:12].

Squad moments

The performances that built the title

Virat Kohli's 76 in the final

The highest score by an Indian in a T20 World Cup final. Rohit said Kohli's knock "helped calm nerves in the dressing room" when India lost three early wickets.

Axar Patel's 47 off 31

Rohit singled out Axar's innings for special praise: "Not many people are talking about Axar's knock, but that innings was a real game-changer."

Bumrah, Hardik & Arshdeep

The pace trio combined to concede just 22 runs in the final five overs, snatching the trophy from South Africa's grasp.

Rahul Dravid's final mission

Rohit dedicated the win to outgoing head coach Rahul Dravid, calling it the perfect farewell after years of service to Indian cricket.

Rohit on the final

"I didn't sleep the whole night. I was only thinking about the World Cup. I was nervous. I couldn't feel my legs. Did I feel nerves? Of course. I just don't show it — but inside, it was a lot."

— Rohit Sharma, 2025
